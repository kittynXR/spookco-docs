Development field notes · AI

Killer baseline

The creator's verdict after the September 14 near-full-lobby playtest was that the killers were too easy: running away always worked, because pursuit ran at 3.25 m/s against a 4.3 m/s player run. The September 15 pressure pass raises pursuit with the crew, adds a close lunge, makes worksite doors part of the chase and re-rolls field obstacles every stop. The September 20 pass adds gradual footsteps, cover attenuation, partial-exposure suspicion and warned lethal commitments. These are accepted starting values, not measured balance.

Movement sensing uses horizontal speed. Ordinary killers ignore speeds up to 0.9 m/s; Rattletrap starts above 0.55 m/s. Radius interpolates from zero to the walking value at 2.7 m/s, then to the running value at 4.3 m/s. Cover multiplies the radius by 0.25 for ordinary hearing and 0.65 for Rattletrap. Detection distance is spherical. Soft Soles multiplies movement ranges by 0.75. Safe, downed/ineligible, and suspended players are excluded. This does not inspect microphone input.

Visible and audible candidates are distinct. Vision takes priority; sound behind cover starts investigation without granting sight-only warnings/attacks. New sound evidence refreshes at most every 0.8 seconds. When a player becomes quiet or leaves hearing range, the last evidence and deadline stop following them.

Rattletrap remains blind, with the largest movement ranges and separate task-noise investigation. Usher investigates after a broken warning and must warn afresh when it visually reacquires.

Sight samples head and torso separately. Full exposure confirms immediately; one visible sample accumulates suspicion for 0.5 seconds, with slow decay behind full cover. Escape Artist (level 10) extends this to 1.1 seconds and scales visual searches to 65%, without shortening sound searches. Trailbreaker separately scales footstep-evidence searches to 65%; it does not shorten visual searches. Repair noise uses cover attenuation and Silent Tools halves its range. Hitchhiker ambush focus uses visible targets or evidence less than six seconds old, never hidden live positions.

Roadkill charges execute. Hitchhiker and Usher warn for two seconds before a six-second lethal commitment against the warned player; breaking sight cancels it. Expiry gives two seconds of recovery and requires a new warning. Session elimination uses synced identities to survive re-registration and leaves the quota seat intact. Actual Udon tests cover these paths; uploaded migration and physical input acceptance remain separate.

Turning corners and searching

Observed movement can project a short continuation, bounded to five metres. Mode 6 searches alternate nearby reachable NavMesh probes with authored route anchors. Missing route anchors no longer cause instant abandonment. Searches have both time and point budgets, so the configured duration is a ceiling rather than a guaranteed wait.

Search pauses sweep left/right for 2.2 seconds through a ±100° range. Moving patrol/search adds bounded lateral scanning (±28° patrol, ±45° search) without stopping navigation. Actual facing drives vision and presentation. Synced checkpoint fields preserve probe position, progress, reference heading, and sensing state.

Speed, lunges and the cost of a hit

Pursuit is a multiple of the players' base run speed and patrol a multiple of their base walk speed (2.7 / 4.3 m/s), and both rise linearly with the crew to +20% at eight or more participants. The crew count is the stop's locked count lowered by departures, so late joiners never speed a hunter up mid-stop.

Values are metres per second, rounded; committed charges and attacks have their own timing. Solo patrol is 2.70 m/s for the original six, 2.97 for Hound, 2.16 for Mimic/Warden and 2.43 for Deadeye, with the same crew factor. Shared melee pursuit has a close lunge and a 1.5-second recovery after a landed ordinary hit. Gun killers use confirmation, committed aim and reload windows rather than contact damage.

Players run 4.3 m/s before items and perks. Faster melee hunters reward cover, doors and interruption; ranged hunters instead make distance, warning timing, sidesteps and solid cover matter. Cargo slows players without Courier. Judge these interactions in real play rather than treating the speed table as a complete difficulty model.

Doors and per-stop layouts

Worksite front openings have hinged doors. Crew open and close them; the owner validates distance, eligibility, a cooldown and whether anyone stands in the doorway or the leaf's swing. Doors deliberately do not carve navigation: a hunter stops at a closed leaf, opens it in 0.8 s when unhurried, and bashes it off its hinge in 1.2 s while pursuing or following fresh evidence — a broken door stays open for the rest of the stop, so a door buys distance once and cannot be looped. Rear openings stay clear as a second exit.

Every stop attempt also rolls a fresh set of field obstacles (hay bales, stalled vehicles, log piles, crates, drums, a trailer). Solid parts carve the navigation mesh at runtime, and after each roll the owner verifies that the configured patrol, cargo, repair and supply destinations remain reachable, re-rolling twice and then falling back to a clear field. The spectator map draws the active obstacles and closed doors over its baked terrain.

Presentation and debug surfaces

The world killer model and spectator map use the same always-active feed. Non-owners wait for a matching pose, snap on the first valid sample/round change, then interpolate nearby updates. Feed data includes warning/hazard/decoy state and hearing ranges.

The map’s gold vision outline is clipped against cover; blue rings show walking/running hearing. The legend also reports the hunter's current speed and crew factor, and the map draws gray blocks for the rolled obstacles and cream bars for closed doors, so a watcher sees the same field the crew is in. Explicit sorting keeps them above terrain. Only eligible dispatch spectators can view tracking. The map is a horizontal projection, so vertical cover and actual head visibility can differ.

Host → AI diagnostics is an optional independent reader for owner IDs, heartbeat age, path state, feed receipt, presentation readiness, and movement information. It now reports Killer v4 with the current speed, landed hits, lunges and doors opened or broken; the owner logs the same line on mode changes and every ten seconds. Mode 7 is “at a door”. Radar visibility and diagnostics are development features; decide whether to remove or restrict them before release.

Specialist encounters

The ten-killer roster retains one active killer. SpookEncounterAI owns specialist evidence and ranged commitment while SpookKiller owns shared navigation, doors, pose and interruptions.

  • Hound records bounded movement breadcrumbs, waits at least 1.5 seconds before investigating them, and expires them after 12 seconds (five with Trailbreaker). It does not refresh its search by rereading one breadcrumb.
  • Mimic uses a separate false-cargo mesh, breathing motion and positional creaks. Proximity reveal requires visible, unsmoked body evidence and a 1.6-second warning; real objective cargo is untouched.
  • Warden confirms sight for 0.45 seconds, warns for 1.8 seconds and locks the last 0.55 seconds. Its 14-metre shotgun fan is lethal and followed by a 3.2-second reload.
  • Deadeye confirms for 0.8 seconds, warns for 2.5 seconds and locks the last 0.7 seconds. Its 38-metre rifle is lethal, reloads for four seconds and selects a reachable lateral firing position when available.

Gun searches use frozen sight/sound positions with a six-second budget. Escape Artist and Trailbreaker shorten the corresponding search by 35 percent. Expiry releases the old navigation path. Decoys and successful stuns clear continuous visual confirmation; accepted repair noise is consumed once. Cover blocks actual shot rays; smoke blocks acquisition and pre-commit aim only. Ownership recovery cancels pending shots instead of replaying them.

Specialist presentation uses attempt-keyed synced arrays for stage, deadlines, muzzle/aim points, shot serial and trails. The receiving-client fixture decodes those arrays through the ClientSim SDK codec, alongside the normal pose feed; uploaded transport and device acceptance are still separate gates.

Deferred work and acceptance

  • Fair search persistence and pressure distribution for solo through 16 participants.
  • Human evasion across multiple corners, noisy work, decoys, and changing routes.
  • Roadkill charge visibility versus actual hits under latency.
  • Hitchhiker warp presentation and safe relocation tells.
  • Switchboard hazard cover rules, warning/active distinction, and alternate escape routes.
  • Unreachable targets with reachable alternatives and owner changes in every mode.
  • Cooldown/stun immunity and strong-perk builds without unavoidable chains.
  • Whether the new speeds are fair rather than merely hard. crewSpeedBonus and the per-killer chaseSpeedRatios are the first inspector values to lower if a chase reads as unavoidable.
  • Door agreement across clients and after owner migration, and the stop-start navigation carve cost on Quest and iPhone.

The local lanes cover navigation, frozen evidence, hearing boundaries/perks, search expiry, synced metadata, and rendered rings/cones. The fresh uploaded fairness and migration matrix remains open.
