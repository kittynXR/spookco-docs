Development notesWORKING BUILD / SEPT 2026

Development field notes · September 15, 2026

Current project status

Playable baseline accepted. Not a release candidate. The creator reports that the basic game works, killers now provide a useful baseline, and Pass Host plus AFK Claim Host have been tested. The next priority is game and environment design; fine AI tuning is deferred until those decisions are clearer.

Three passes landed on September 15, all of them from the September 14 near-full-lobby playtest: the VR playtest fixes (trip resume, dark transit, upright cargo, selling, a reworked VR panel), the killer pressure pass (crew-scaled pursuit, a close lunge, interactive worksite doors, per-stop field obstacles), and the briefing van hold with spread spawn spots. Everything in them is locally checked and none of it has been played by more than one real client yet — the chase now has to be judged by people, not fixtures.

These notes are unlisted, public documentation. They are not linked from the player navigation or public search index. Do not put credentials, private playtester details, or raw logs here. Development-only spectator radar and AI diagnostics may change or be removed.

How to read the status

Implemented means the code and scene contain the feature. Locally checked means editor/static/ClientSim evidence exists. User confirmed identifies a reported uploaded playtest. Open means unfinished work or missing acceptance. These labels are deliberately not interchangeable.

Baseline snapshot

Game checkpoint: 9a52009 — Merge briefing hold and spread spawns with the killer pressure pass. Unity 2022.3.22f1, VRChat SDK 3.10.5, AI Navigation 1.1.5. Do not upgrade the project to obtain Unity 6 tooling.

The latest local run contains 1,519 passing assertions across structural scene validation and thirteen ClientSim lanes, each lane in its own fresh session on the merged tree that carries both September 15 passes together. That is useful regression evidence, not 1,519 real multiplayer scenarios. See testing and release gates for exact lanes, dates, build sizes, and limits.

Design work comes next

  1. Play the new pressure with real crews before touching its numbers again. If a chase reads as unavoidable rather than hard, the crew speed bonus and the per-killer chase ratios are the first inspector values to lower.
  2. Establish the rhythm of a stop: safe planning, uncertain search, readable pursuit, escape, and a short breather. The briefing hold and the per-stop obstacle roll are first attempts at the planning and search halves of that.
  3. Redesign the two graybox layouts around that rhythm, especially solo routes and the approach to the refuge.
  4. Define the van/campfire and six killer silhouettes before investing in detailed props.
  5. Play the core objectives repeatedly with small and large groups before changing AI numbers again.
  6. Keep regression coverage and record every new uploaded test with build, devices, party size, and result.

Detailed systems, AI, environment/art, roadmap, and publishing instructions are maintained separately.
