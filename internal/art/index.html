Development field notes · Design

Environment & art direction

The current worlds are functional grayboxes with original placeholder geometry and synthesized sounds. The next pass should make the trip worth playing repeatedly before investing in fine AI tuning.

Campfire as a gameplay anchor

The protective lantern and covered van refuge are the dependable destination in every stop. Make their warm silhouette readable from escape routes. Preserve generous access and standing room for the crew; do not turn the entrance into a narrow collision trap.

The van body is currently solid and the protected play space is its covered exterior. A walkable interior is an art/layout decision still to make. Keep exterior capacity even if an interior is added.

Two locations to develop

Area Existing foundation Next design questions

Last Chance Service Station Pumps, canopy/garage/store and vehicle cover How do open forecourt risk and close service corridors differ? Can every job be approached from more than one direction?

Aim for roughly 15–25 seconds of travel from the van to ordinary work sites. This is a design target to measure, not a claim that every current route meets it. Compact solo routes should remain interesting; larger crews need additional useful work sites, not just more repeated walking.

Per-stop layouts and doors

Each area has a Randomized layout root. Its children are sites; each site's children are the mutually exclusive variants one stop can roll — hay rows, stacked bales, stalled vehicles, fallen pines, log stacks, crates and pallets, a tractor, oil drums, a jackknifed trailer, a collapsed sign, tire stacks. The seeded content is graybox: 11 sites with 29 variants across both areas. Replace their meshes freely and add sites of your own; any geometry works, with solid parts on layer 11.

Per site, emptyWeight sets how often nothing appears, variantWeights follow child order, and a positive routeGroup keeps at least one site of that group clear so several blockers can cross one corridor without sealing it. Keep obstacles at least 1.2 m clear of anchors and patrol points and 3 m clear of the refuge — the validator enforces it.

Worksite front openings carry a hinged door: a frame, a leaf that swings inward, and an interaction trigger. Keep the leaf a single solid collider under the hinge and the doorway at least 1.2 m wide; the killer's agent is 0.9 m across. Rear openings must stay clear — supply anchors sit in them, and a cabin whose only two exits could both close is a dead end.

After any of this, run SpookCo → Apply gameplay scene additions, which adds carving obstacles, keeps the new geometry out of the navigation bake and the spectator map texture, rebuilds navigation when static geometry changed, and re-collects the layout. Then run Validate scene and rules, which fails on a site with no variants, a variant that would block an anchor or the refuge, a mis-wired door, and a doorway the baked mesh cannot pass through. Re-bake lighting after adding static geometry such as door frames.

Objective and round design

  • Make cargo destinations, repair states, shared supplies, and extraction unmistakable through shape and feedback, not color alone.
  • Retain interrupted repair progress and a soloable path through every mandatory task.
  • Give leaving the refuge a reason: visible jobs, optional supplies, and short risk/reward decisions.
  • Measure the 20/150/30/10-second phase rhythm with real players before changing timers.
  • Test whether three stops produce variety through placement and killer combinations rather than repetitive chores.
  • Preserve at least two plausible responses to ordinary pursuit; avoid compulsory dead ends and unavoidable spawn attacks.

Art sessions

1 — Van and campfire: distressed company paint, protective lantern, awning, broad access, warm safe silhouette. Preserve zone, arrival, interaction, and navigation transforms.

2 — Killer identities: six strong silhouettes on a shared modest rig foundation. Keep visual weapon reach consistent with the simulation. Current procedural limb animation expects named arms/boots; replace deliberately if adding Animator rigs. Preserve attack warnings and hazard readability.

3 — Environment and sound: a modular cabin/garage kit, canopy, low-poly trees, shared atlas, and clear objective props. Replace synthesized warnings and decoy clicks with short original sounds; add restrained ambience and fire. Check voice-chat intelligibility and phone speakers.

Asset budgets and authoring guardrails

Current recorded geometry is 64,686 triangles across all loaded meshes, which includes the graybox layout variants and door frames. Initial mobile targets are below 70 MB download and about 250,000 visible world triangles; these are provisional, not frame-rate evidence.

Start with a shared 1024-pixel atlas and simple materials. Prefer baked lighting, few transparent layers, limited particles/rigs/physics, and pooled supplies. There are 24 authored supply anchors per area; preserve compact solo eligibility and distinct reachable locations.

Do not move protected zones, objective anchors, supply anchors, killer spawn points, patrol routes, the sixteen van and dispatch spawn spots, or the briefing barrier and its glowing marker accidentally when replacing meshes. Structural changes require navigation/lighting rebakes, scene validation, and all three platform builds. Add authors/licenses to credits when importing outside assets.
