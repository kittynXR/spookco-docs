Your first shift

Development build: art, balance, and features are still changing. Spectator radar and AI diagnostics are temporary development features, not promises for the finished game.

Content warning: Contains darkness, flickering lights and sudden loud sounds (gunshots).

Your job: finish the shared contract, get back to the van, then return to dispatch. Each round is one contract. Bring back orange cargo and fuel/repair green generators to finish both quotas. Contribute for the contract bonus; extract alive for more. Work rewards are kept if the contract fails, but that round's XP remains subject to extraction penalties.

Remember three rewards: cash buys supplies for this live session; XP earns levels and perk points; commendations permanently unlock reusable gear. Survival points is the old name for commendations. Career titles and survivor field notes are longer-term goals you can explore after your first shift.

New to the company? Read About SpookCo for the contracts, dispatch and the spooks.

1. Get ready at dispatch

Wait for saved progress to load. On your first visit, choose English or Japanese; the choice is saved and can be changed under Controls → i18n / Language. This is separate from the website's language switch.

New arrivals start not ready. Click Ready / Unready to opt in. Your choice stays on between rounds until you unready; Watch / Withdraw also opts out. Your button shows your own ready status alongside the shared ready count.

The game host clicks Start next round with at least one player ready. A five-second countdown appears on the ready controls. Only ready players depart; the host may remain unready and spectate. Other players can click the start control to ready/unready. Its host name follows game-host transfers, independently of VRChat master status. The office Host panel also offers Start.

Find Controls & Accessibility beside the ready board. Outside the roll-up door, on the right, is the free practice bay. Lit billboards stand across the low yard fence in the vacant lot, facing it. They carry full-size field and industrial generator exhibits, diagrams for all three startup games and a loadout poster (a sign over the exit points the way). These are visual references, not interactive machines, and cannot consume fuel. The practice bay has three startup tasks, free soda, tape and oil, and a crate to carry and throw. None of it touches your saved supplies or rewards. A generator you finish clears away for you until you press the Reset tutorial post by the bench twice. Leaving the bay puts the practice supplies back.

2. Read the contract

Each round begins with a 15-second briefing for the contract, shop and loadout. The destination and spook are rolled for each new round.

You may leave the van at any second of the briefing. Your status shows BRIEFING — shop at the van or head out (Ns) and your status readout counts down, so the crew can read the contract and shop together before heading out — or go at once. Shopping and selling at the van's side-door boards work the whole briefing; when the expedition starts those boards go blank until the next briefing.

3. Head into the field

The expedition lasts 150–240 seconds depending on crew size. Briarwood High School gives every crew at least seven minutes. Carry orange cargo cases back to the van and repair green equipment. Repairs retain progress when interrupted. Every required objective can be done alone without a purchased item or specific perk.

At Briarwood High School, follow the hallway loop around the courtyard and use the four marked stairs to reach the basement plant. The cafeteria, gym, auditorium, library and basement stay open at every crew size. Classrooms open in groups: 9 for 1–2 players, 18 for 3–4, 27 for 5–7, and all 36 for 8 or more. Check the ground/B1 tabs on the van map.

Generators start dry. Small field generators need one can; industrial diesel generators need two. Find cans around the unlocked map and carry them against the machine: each can stays attached. Industrial controls unlock only after both cans arrive. Every round spawns two or three spare cans beyond its actual requirement.

Solo and duo rounds use one small generator. Crews of 3–8 get at least one of each size; 9–12 get at least two small and one industrial; 13–16 get at least two of each. Remaining choices and their positions are randomized when the round starts.

A short pole marks the machine's state: slow flashing red means empty, solid red means one of two cans, orange means startup required, and pale green means running. Minigame panels have a translucent backing so movement behind them stays visible. Use the controls on the front to complete one shuffled startup task:

  • Prime and purge: pump until the fuel chamber fills, then bleed the trapped air. Clear three pockets and press Start Engine. Extra pumping and early bleeding make noise.
  • Listen and tune: adjust choke and throttle until both read Steady, then press Start Engine. The sound and visual meters give the same information.
  • Route the fuel: turn three valves to connect the top-left supply to the bottom-right engine inlet, then press Start Engine. More than one route works.

The first three fueled generators draw all three games in a random order. A task stays assigned and keeps its progress when you step away. One person operates a generator at a time; someone else can take over after they leave or stop working. Mistakes attract the spook from farther away. Mechanic and tool oil make the controls more forgiving, and repair tape fixes one task step without skipping the final start.

Finishing a generator also powers nearby active gear: stay within six metres with no solid wall between you and the machine to recharge twice as fast. It keeps working during extraction. The machine label and your HUD/wrist show the benefit; spooks can still reach you there.

Players joining the world wait for the next round and do not raise the active quota. Departures and withdrawals can lower requirements while preserving finished work. Downed players and teammates sheltering at the van still count.

The field is not the same twice. Every stop rolls fresh obstacles — hay bales, broken-down vehicles, fallen trees, crates and drums — alongside the usual random supplies, and worksite front doors can be opened and closed while you work. See know the spooks for how the spook deals with a door you shut behind you.

4. Regroup and extract

The covered campfire refuge beside the van is protected throughout the stop. Enter and leave freely. You can walk into the van through its side opening, the back or a cab door (click a cab door's handle or window to open or close it); the bench, console and doors inside do not block you, and the van is part of the protected refuge.

Finishing the quota starts extraction early, with 60–90 seconds depending on the crew-size tier, or at least 90 seconds at Briarwood. Running out of expedition time triggers emergency extraction instead. Get back before departure: it does not wait indefinitely for missing players. Transit takes 10 seconds: the view fades to black over one second and stays dark with a short next-stop message while walking pauses, then fades in at the next van. Standing avatars are never driven around. Menus cannot be opened during transit. The crew arrives spread around the van on separate spots rather than stacked on one point, and the same applies whenever the game moves you — a restart, a recovery, or returning to dispatch.

After a completed contract, gold-strapped BONUS SALVAGE cases become available near the van's surrounding work area: one for an initial crew of 1–2, two for 3–8, or three for 9–16. Bring them back using the normal cargo controls. Each pays double normal cargo XP and $24, and a real handoff shares credit. They are optional and never raise the required quota.

The extraction clock keeps running. The crew gets an eight-second decision window before the usual all-aboard departure check starts; everyone can stay aboard and skip the salvage. Unfinished bonus cases never hold the van past its deadline. Failed contracts unlock no bonus work, and restarting a stop never replenishes a bonus case that already paid.

Missing extraction keeps only 10% of your round XP. Each dispatched player left behind also reduces everyone's round XP by 10%, capped at 50%; previous rounds' XP stays untouched.

One completed contract finishes the round. A short result shows the outcome and your cash, XP and commendations, then everyone returns to dispatch. Staying safe without contributing does not earn the contract bonus. Players who remain ready join the next round when the host starts it.

Lethal attacks

Roadkill's charge, warned catches from Hitchhiker and Usher, and gunshots from Warden and Deadeye eliminate you for the rest of the round, even with Hardhat or Second Wind. You drop what you are carrying and can spectate at dispatch. If everyone is eliminated, the stop ends promptly. Your banked rewards remain. See spook counterplay for warnings and escape routes.

If you go down

You normally start with two health. A short immunity interval prevents immediate repeated hits. Going down puts you on the floor: you crawl slowly, you cannot jump, and you drop whatever you were carrying. Your first down of a stop gives you 20 seconds; every later down in the same stop gives you 10.

Getting within 2 metres of a downed teammate automatically starts rescuing them. Stay within 4 metres to continue; each rescuer helps one teammate at a time. It works with cargo or equipment in hand and with menus open, without pressing a button. One rescuer takes 10 seconds, and each extra rescuer takes a quarter off that — 7.5 seconds for two, 5.6 for three. Field Medic halves the crew's time. Your bleed-out countdown stops while anyone contributes. Leaving range resumes the countdown immediately; rescue progress survives for 2.5 seconds, then resets and adds half the invested time back to the countdown. Camp-AFK teammates can rescue you too; suspended players cannot.

If no able teammate remains, including solo play, your countdown is cut to a few seconds rather than run in full — nothing can answer it, but you still get that long on the floor. Otherwise, when your countdown expires, what happens depends on when you went down. In the first half of a stop an eight-second recovery brings you round at the nearest recovery stone in your ring — a small glowing gravestone — when it is closer than the van, otherwise at the van. The stones vanish at the halfway point and take nobody after it. After halfway you are out for the rest of that stop: you return to dispatch, can watch the live field map, and join the next host-started round if you remain ready. Dropped cargo can be recovered either way. Capture never removes banked XP or commendations. Second Wind allows a six-second field recovery on the first ordinary down of a stop; it cannot revive an elimination.

Joining a round already in progress

New arrivals stay at dispatch and start unready. Ready / Unready during a round sets your choice for the next departure; it does not join or withdraw you from the active contract. Unreadying in the field lets you finish this round and sit out the next. Your ready choice lasts until you change it or leave the instance.
