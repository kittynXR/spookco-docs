Know the spooks

One spook is active at a time. Melee spooks keep pace with a running employee and gain speed with larger crews. Warden and Deadeye move more slowly, but threaten firing lanes at a distance. Read the warning and use each spook's recovery window.

Spook What it does What to try
Groundskeeper Sight pursuit, remembered routes, and local searches Break sight, change route, and quietly leave its search area.
Rattletrap Blind; investigates movement and task noise Walk when possible, time repairs, and use a decoy.
Roadkill Telegraphs a lethal, committed charge Dodge sideways and put solid cover between you and its line.
Hitchhiker Ambushes from concealed positions; warned catches are lethal Watch roadside silhouettes and change approach.
Usher Warns before a lethal chase from its vision cone; drawn to flashlight beams it can see Get out of the cone or behind cover before the lock completes. Switch your light off when it is near.
Switchboard Pursues and marks work-site/route hazards Leave the marked area and switch sites.
Hound Follows aging movement trails as well as sight Creep, double back, and leave the old trail behind. Trailbreaker helps.
Mimic Disguises itself as false cargo, then warns before revealing Watch for eyes, twitching and creaks. Back away; Inspector can identify it.
Warden Warns, commits to a shotgun fan, fires, then reloads Leave the fan or reach solid cover. Move while it reloads.
Deadeye Quickly confirms sight, raises its rifle with a warning sound, commits and fires; spots flashlight beams from far away Break sight immediately or sidestep before the shot. It changes position between shots. Keep your beam out of its view.

Your footsteps matter

Creeping is quiet for ordinary spooks. Footsteps grow louder gradually as you move faster, and solid cover greatly reduces their reach. Rattletrap is the hearing specialist: it can detect slower movement and hear more through cover. Running is much riskier than walking. Soft Soles reduces movement hearing ranges by 25%.

A brief head-only or body-only peek builds suspicion before a sighted spook confirms you. Repeated peeks accumulate suspicion, and showing both your head and torso confirms you immediately. Once a spook is tracking you, a currently visible head or torso is enough to maintain a recent chase. Full cover still breaks sight, and cover does not erase a route the spook already saw.

This senses in-game movement and certain task events. It does not listen to microphone audio or voice chat.

Flashlights

Switching a flashlight makes a click that a spook within earshot hears, much like a footstep, and solid cover muffles it. Switch in cover or away from the spook. The Usher and Deadeye also notice a lit beam they can see: the patch of wall or floor it lights, or the lamp itself pointed their way. They come for whoever holds it. A passing glimpse is not enough, but a beam that stays in their view for a moment is. Focused Beam's switch is silent to spooks, but its beam is still light.

Hiding in shrubs

Dense shrubs conceal you from ordinary spook vision and reduce your walking, running and sideways movement speed by 50% while your body is inside. Leaving restores your normal speed. The slowdown stacks with carried cargo, perks and temporary effects.

Break sight before entering. A spook actively chasing or aiming at you can keep tracking you if it watches you enter. If you break its view, the shrub conceals you until you expose yourself or it comes over to inspect your hiding place. Standing on top of foliage does not count as hiding.

Keep your flashlight off or pointed away from the spook. A head lamp or hand light shining directly at a sighted spook reveals you through foliage when you are within its view and range. Once it sees you, switching the light off alone does not hide you again: break its sight. Solid cover and smoke still conceal you, and Rattletrap remains blind.

A bush can be searched. After losing you in a chase, a spook checks nearby bushes around the route or open area it reached. It walks over and pauses to inspect them; a sighted spook can discover you when it looks into your bush at close range. It can check empty bushes too, and still gives up when its search expires. Watch where it goes and quietly move away before it checks your hiding place.

Bushes do not suppress hearing, erase Hound trails, stop committed bullets or charges, or make you invulnerable. Rattletrap remains a hearing specialist. Quiet movement and a route change still matter.

Cars, trailers, equipment and wooden fences provide solid cover. Chain-link fencing blocks passage while allowing sight through it; plan around its openings.

One corner is not a guaranteed escape

A spook that has seen you commits to you. For as long as the commitment lasts it sees you farther and wider, hears your footsteps and your flashlight click through cover, and prefers you over a teammate who happens to be nearer. When you break its sight it does not stop at the corner: it runs to where it last saw you, then around the turn your route was taking, and only after that does it search, quickly and at full pace, around that corner. Keep running after the turn and your footsteps re-aim it. It never knows where you actually are once you are out of sight; it guesses from the way you were going and from what it can hear.

It tires. About 20 to 32 seconds after its last sighting, depending on the spook, with every sound of yours pushing that a few seconds further, it gives up, recovers, and goes back to its rounds. Warden and Deadeye commit too; Rattletrap commits to the first footsteps it locates; the Hound follows your scent around the corner. Quietly escaping still works: creep, take a second turn it did not see, or double back, and let it tire. Escape Artist shortens a commitment made by sight; Trailbreaker shortens one made by sound and makes Hound trails fade sooner. The refuge and a decoy end it outright; a stun or a flash only interrupts it. An uncommitted search (from a noise it heard, say) is shorter and slower than a committed one.

Break sight, change route, reduce noise, and use the protected refuge when needed. Sound gives the AI an investigation point; it is not treated as direct vision through a wall.

Running in a straight line is unreliable against melee spooks. They lead their turns instead of trailing you, and inside about three metres they can lunge. A landed hit costs them a moment's recovery, which is the gap you use to reach cover, a door or the van. Carrying a case slows you below their pace: drop it if you are being run down.

Reaching the van ends the hunt

Make it into the van's protected area with a spook behind you and it gives up on you. Within a moment it turns away from the camp and heads back out into the field, searching as it goes: anyone it spots or hears out there is fair game, and that is who it chases next. For about 15 seconds after you get in, anyone within about 12 m of the camp, roughly the edge of the lantern's glow, goes unnoticed and cannot be struck, so the crew can regroup and step straight back out. Farther out you are fair game as usual.

Spooks do not loiter at the camp. Their rounds and searches never stop inside the lantern's glow, and for about a minute after an escape their rounds keep well clear of the van. They still come close when they are chasing someone they can see or hear, and a teammate downed near the van is left after a short look, so they can be rescued.

Lethal attacks

Roadkill's charge, a warned catch from Hitchhiker or Usher, and a hit from Warden or Deadeye eliminate you for the rest of the round. Hardhat and Second Wind cannot save you from these attacks. Spawn and rescue immunity still protect you. Hitchhiker and Usher must warn before committing; breaking sight cancels their lethal chase, and they must warn again for a full two seconds when they find you again, even mid-chase. A missed commitment gives you a recovery window. Solid cover stops contact through walls.

You can spectate from dispatch, and you join the next host-started round if you remain ready. If the whole crew is eliminated, the stop ends promptly. Eliminated players do not reduce the required task quota, and reconnecting or toggling readiness does not undo elimination.

Reading a gun warning

React to the raised weapon and warning sound immediately. Both gun spooks now acquire and fire quickly, with no visible aiming rays or shot tracers. Their aim follows you briefly, then fixes before firing: a late sidestep can still avoid the shot. Solid cover blocks the actual shot, including after commitment. Smoke can break acquisition or an uncommitted aim, but it cannot stop a bullet already aimed at your position.

Decoys redirect an uncommitted armed spook; they cannot cancel its warning once aiming starts. A successful flash or trip alarm interrupts it and makes it confirm you again. Warden must reload after its shotgun blast. Deadeye reloads and looks for a new firing position. Neither deals melee contact damage.

False cargo and old trails

Mimic cargo is separate from real objectives. It never takes away delivered work. Its breathing, eyes and twitch are clues before the reveal; Inspector identifies a nearby visible disguise. Smoke can conceal your approach, and the reveal warning gives you time to back away.

The Hound follows recorded movement only after a delay. Quiet creeping leaves no new trail; old samples expire instead of tracking your hidden position. It can still see you normally, so use cover as you leave the trail.

Doors, and a field that changes every stop

Worksite front doors can be opened and closed. Shut one behind you and the spook has to deal with it: an unhurried one opens it, but a spook that is chasing you breaks it off its hinge, and a broken door cannot be closed again. A door buys you distance once, so spend it well. Nobody can close a door on a person standing in the doorway, and the rear opening of every worksite stays clear so you are never sealed in.

The field itself is rolled again at the start of every stop: hay bales, broken-down vehicles, fallen trees, crates, oil drums and more appear in different places, alongside the usual random supplies. Learn the landmarks, not the route.

Spectating the current build

Choose Watch / Withdraw to remain at dispatch. The Live Field Map shows crew and spook tracking, with every crew member named beside their dot. In the current test build, a gold outline depicts vision and blue rings depict walking/running hearing; Rattletrap has hearing only. The dispatch map always plots the spook for spectators, before the crew has identified it. Tap a name, a dot, the spook arrow or the roster in the legend column and the screen on the office window follows that player or the spook over the shoulder (PC only for now). The crew's own van map plots teammates by name too, but shows the spook only during extraction or in the open near the van after a sighting. Tap a teammate on the van map and the security CRT on the van's driver-side wall follows them the same way (PC only for now); the spook appears in that view only while the van map shows it.

Development feature: visible sensing radar and AI diagnostics may change or disappear from the final game. The map is a simplified horizontal view, not a guarantee of detection at every height or through every shape of cover.
