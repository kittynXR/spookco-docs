Controls & accessibility

Use the Controls & Accessibility panel beside dispatch’s ready board. You can also reach it from the van and personal inventory. Preferences are saved separately for desktop, VR, and touch. The Controls side rail groups them into Input & Interaction, Menu, Placement & HUD, Flashlight, Audio, and Language. It shows settings relevant to your current input device and remembers your last category during the session.

Choose your language

On a first visit, the game waits for your saved profile, then shows a bilingual English/Japanese picker if no supported language is saved. On desktop, press 1 for English or 2 for Japanese in that initial picker, or hold Tab and click. In VR, use the personal-menu pointer on the floating picker.

Change it later under Controls → Language. The choice is saved across visits and shared between your desktop and VR profiles; resetting controls does not reset language. It changes only your own view, so the crew can use different languages. Website language links do not change your in-world setting. Branding, legal notices, and developer diagnostics retain their original text.

A Tab peek does not replace the page remembered by I. For example, close Controls, peek at Inventory with Tab, then press I: Controls returns to the category you selected. Holding Tab on an already open menu enables clicking without changing pages. Shift+Tab also opens the menu while sprint is held; Alt/Control chords are ignored. Native cursor and movement behavior remains controlled by VRChat.

The six numbered shortcuts work without Tab, including with Shift held. They can be rebound, and conflicting bindings are reported. Reserve selection swaps the chosen reserve into the equipped slot; it does not draw or consume it. Press 2 to draw, then make a fresh Use input. Choose Controls → Menu → When Tab is released to close it or keep it open. If you bind an item action to I, that binding takes precedence and the menu hint omits I.

VR: wrist or floating panel

Flick the right stick up or down to put the next item in your hand: hands free, your gear, then each consumable you carry. Pull the trigger on that hand to use it. Nothing needs confirming and no menu needs opening. At dispatch, hold the stick up to ready up or unready: a three second count appears across your view and letting go early cancels it (Controls → Input & Interaction → Ready hold turns this back into a quick half-second hold). Hold the stick down for about half a second to open the menu. If your controller's stick does not register up or down, hold the jump button for about half a second instead: it opens and closes the same menu, whose READY / JOIN button readies you up, and the ready post by the ramp does the same by touch. The first time you arrive at dispatch in VR, a short banner across your view says so. Choose either wrist and either equipment hand. There is no button on the wrist, and opening the menu does not freeze movement.

Floating mode is the default. The panel appears once, level in front of you, at the moment you open it, and switching tabs never moves it again. If you open it while looking up at a sign or down at the floor it slides toward where you are looking, and it pulls in front of any wall or board closer than its set distance. The first time it opens for you, a short banner across your view says where it is. Wrist mode instead keeps the panel attached to your chosen wrist, so it travels with your arm. Its height, distance, size and angle are set on the Placement & HUD page (a category on the Controls rail; see below), and Aim at me there turns it to face your eyes from wherever you are holding your hand: hold your arm the way you read it and press it with the other hand. All of it is saved with your profile.

The floating panel closes itself when you move more than three metres from where it was placed, when the expedition starts, when you pick up cargo, and when you take a hit or go down. If it ends up out of reach, close it with the same stick or jump hold and open it again: a fresh panel appears in front of you. Aim at me on the Placement / HUD page does the same. Menus cannot be opened during transit between stops.

Buttons that duplicate VRChat's own controls are hidden unless the current situation needs them, so the panel stays compact. The status readout card (the round, your cash, health and level, anything acting on you (soda, Adrenaline, slowed, fog, EMF bearing) and what is in your hand, on up to five lines) sits small in the lower-left corner of your view during a trip (the briefing, the stop and the van ride): about a metre ahead, following your head's turn with a slight lag and pulled in front of walls. At dispatch it rides your wrist instead and stays hidden unless Controls → Menu → Wrist display at dispatch is shown. An alert brings it up anywhere and turns the card red. On the wrist it steps aside while the menu is open on the same wrist. Choose Placement & HUD → Wrist readout → Size to set the card's size.

Placement & HUD (a category on the Controls rail, office board or menu) moves things to where you can see them, one element at a time: the menu panel, the wrist readout, the item in your hand, the alert banner and the hand torch. Pick the element with Next element, the axis with Next axis (offset, tilt, size, or a HUD cell), and step it with - / +; the page shows the numbers as you go, and while it is open the readout (and the item or torch you are placing) stays visible, even at dispatch (on the wrist it stays off beside a wrist-mode menu, where the card would cover the page). Next anchor cycles the readout between AUTO (the default: the HUD corner during a trip, your wrist at dispatch), HEAD (HUD) everywhere and WRIST everywhere. On AUTO the page shows and moves the corner spot (keep your head at the page's centre to see the card beside it), and WRIST lets you set the wrist pose. For the held item it switches hand / head, and for the menu panel floating / wrist. Pinned elements sit in a cell of a 9 x 7 grid about a metre ahead, pull in front of walls, trail your head's turn a little (HUD follow locks them instead) and on PC hide while VRChat's camera is out; the HUD frame element sets how wide and tall that grid is. The alert banner always trails. Your item placement is only for you: other players still see items in the normal hand pose. On desktop the VR-only elements say so, and the HUD frame is the desktop HUD scale; on touch you place the alert banner. Reset element and Reset all placement put things back; everything is saved with your profile for each input type.

Personal items follow the chosen hand. Cargo uses normal pickup controls, or the accessible Carry / Drop actions. If a native pickup occupies the equipment hand, stow or move it deliberately; personal equipment must not forcibly release it.

Generator controls and rescue

In VR, tap the translucent rectangle on a generator control with either hand. Each tap performs one action; move your hand away before tapping again. Holding your hand in the control does not repeat it. Point-and-trigger also works, and desktop/touch players keep their normal point-and-select controls. Touch and trigger together count as one press.

The flashing beacon identifies an unfueled generator. The nearby panel shows its fuel count: field generators need one can, industrial generators two. Once fueled, complete the shown startup task and press Start Engine. Instructions, meters, and feedback stay on the panel; there is no overhead generator label. Practice uses the same controls.

Revives happen automatically when an eligible teammate stays close enough to a downed player. There is no held repair/rescue mode or Cancel repair button to configure.

Flashlight

Every crew member carries a light into the field. By default it is a head lamp that follows your gaze, so the beam lights whatever you look at, hands free. Controls → Flashlight adds a hand light on the right or left hand instead of, or as well as, the head lamp; in VR it points where that controller points, on desktop it follows your view. Both choices are saved with your profile. In VR, tap the trigger of your Light switch hand in the field to switch every beam you carry on or off. Controls → Flashlight → Light switch hand picks it: Other hand (the default, the hand that is not holding your equipment), Left trigger or Right trigger. If you pick your equipment hand, a tap switches the light only while no item is drawn; with an item out, that trigger uses the item. Holding a crate or gas can in the switch hand still lets you switch, and so does pointing an empty switch hand across something you are holding or carrying. With loot in one hand and a crate or gas can in the other, the loot hand's trigger stores the loot and the other trigger switches the light; with loot in your switch hand and the other hand free, the other trigger switches too while nothing is drawn in it. Storing loot never changes either hand setting. A held trigger does nothing to the light, and neither does a press that grabs, operates a generator or clicks a button, or any press while the menu is open. On desktop, F switches the light, and Controls → Flashlight → Flashlight power works everywhere. Lights are off at dispatch and come on when the stop begins; pressing a lamp button at dispatch previews your beams for a few seconds so you can see where they sit (only you see it; closing the menu keeps it, and it ends when you walk off, push a stick, change tab or open the menu again). In VR a hand light also puts a small torch in that hand, and in Controls → Flashlight, Hand beam orientation turns the beam 90 degrees out of the bottom (pinky side) or top (thumb side) of your fist so you can hold it at the shoulder (its offset moves the torch model, never the beam); desktop hand lights aim with your view and ignore the flip. Everyone in the instance sees everyone else's beams, and VR players' torches, including where a teammate is pointing.

The switch makes an audible click-clack. Nearby players hear it, and so does a spook within earshot, much as it would hear a footstep. The Focused Beam perk (level 15) throws a narrower beam about two thirds farther, and its damped switch is quieter and silent to spooks. The Usher and Deadeye can still see its beam.

Phone and tablet

Use the screen’s MENU, USE ITEM, and COLLECT / INTERACT buttons. The world-space lobby boards are also present, but the screen controls are the intended touch shortcut. Inside inventory, select equipment, consumables, or reserves; use the separate Use action to activate the selected item.

The iPhone build has been tried by the creator. Final touch layout and ergonomics still need broader device testing.

Preferences and practice

  • Accessible cargo carrying with the same reservation, penalty, delivery, and drop rules.
  • Wrist/equipment handedness, the VR Light switch hand, the wrist HUD size, the ready hold count, and on the Placement & HUD page the placement of the menu panel, wrist readout, held item, alert banner and hand torch, plus the HUD frame (desktop: HUD scale).
  • Audio (on the Controls rail): four levels, Main, SFX, Ambience and Music, each a slider with - / + buttons. 50% is the game as mixed, 100% adds a little headroom, 0% mutes; Main scales the other three. The levels are saved with your profile and shared by desktop, VR and touch, Reset controls & placement leaves them alone, and VRChat's own World volume still applies on top. Menu clicks follow the SFX level while you adjust it, and at dispatch a wind sample plays while the page is open so you can judge Ambience.
  • Desktop key rebinding with conflict feedback and reset defaults.
  • Separate persisted control profiles for each input family.
  • Stick item select, on by default in VR: neutral deadzone 0.25, activation threshold 0.70, dwell 150 ms. Return to neutral before another step; each step equips outright.

These settings control SpookCo actions, not VRChat’s native movement bindings. The free practice bay is open outside the roll-up door, on the right, with the lit generator guide boards across the yard fence in the vacant lot, facing the building; practice supplies spend no real items or cash. Generator tutorial progress is saved across visits; Reset tutorial clears that practice progress. A practice generator you finish clears away for you; the Reset tutorial post at the end of the practice bench (stand right at it and press twice) brings them all back. Walking out of the bay puts the practice supplies and crate back, and the shop always shows your real items. Selecting or collecting an item never activates it automatically. The lobby's two big boards stand in the dispatch office, front right of spawn.

After you finish all three practice tasks, the training boards, Practice Bay sign, and Reset Tutorial sign/button dim together while you are away from the guide boards. Approaching the guide boards brightens them again; the reset button remains usable.

Move the VR readout by hand

Open Controls → Placement & HUD → Move by hand. The gold status card parks within reach. Grip it with an empty hand, move it, and let go on your other wrist or in front of your view. A brighter gold cue means it will dock on the wrist. Your menu keeps its own wrist setting. To swap wrists, first release the card in view, then carry it with the other hand. If a very large avatar places it beyond the supported wrist offset, move it closer to that wrist and release again.

The card's hand-set view direction stays put when you resize the HUD frame. Using Column or Row snaps it back to the grid before stepping. Reset element returns the readout to following the menu wrist. Press Move by hand again to finish; it also ends after 30 seconds idle, when you travel or go down, or when a round phase changes. Desktop and touch use the page's minus/plus controls.

Reduce flicker, under Controls → Input & Interaction, calms recurring environment and HUD pulses, including the Unlisted effect. It starts off. Your choice is saved and shared across desktop, VR and touch, and Reset controls & placement preserves it. Brief interaction cues and natural particle fades remain.
